Courses I teach or supervise at KU Leuven, with links to the official programme pages.

### Planetary Systems (Master 1–2)

Formation and evolution of planetary systems, from solar-system dynamics to exoplanets, debris disks, and astrobiology. 6 ECTS. <a target="_blank" href="https://onderwijsaanbod.kuleuven.be/syllabi/e/G0I52AE.htm#activetab=doelstellingen_idp1983744">KU Leuven website</a>

<ol>
    <li><strong>The two-body problem</strong> — N-body notions, elliptical motion, and the orbit in space.</li>
    <li><strong>Overview of the solar system</strong> — orbital elements of the planets and major satellites, smaller bodies, and the Earth–Moon system.</li>
    <li><strong>Solar-system debris</strong> — small planets, the Kuiper belt, comets, meteorites, and ring systems.</li>
    <li><strong>Origin of the solar system</strong> — observational constraints, planet accretion, chemical diagnostics, and open questions.</li>
    <li><strong>Exosolar planets</strong> — detection methods, census, systematics of exoplanetary systems and their host stars, formation and evolution, and observational prospects.</li>
    <li><strong>Debris disks</strong> — detection, census, formation and evolution, and their relation to planetary systems.</li>
    <li><strong>Astrobiology</strong> — complex chemistry in space, solar-system exploration, planetary atmospheres, the biosphere, and prospects for detecting extraterrestrial life.</li>
</ol>

### Observational Techniques in Astronomy (Master 1–2)

Physical and statistical bases of modern astronomical instrumentation, from coordinate systems and photometry to telescopes, spectroscopy, interferometry, and detectors. Students complete two projects (SED modelling and HERMES data analysis). 6 ECTS. <a target="_blank" href="https://onderwijsaanbod.kuleuven.be/syllabi/e/G0I45AE.htm#activetab=doelstellingen_idp1079968">KU Leuven website</a>

<ul>
    <li><strong>L1</strong> — General introduction and course content</li>
    <li><strong>L2</strong> — Introduction: coordinate systems, velocity, and time</li>
    <li><strong>L3</strong> — Introduction: magnitudes, photometry, and extinction</li>
    <li><strong>P1</strong> — Project 1: SED modelling</li>
    <li><strong>L4</strong> — Observing techniques: telescopes and telescope optics</li>
    <li><strong>L5</strong> — Observing techniques: telescope instruments</li>
    <li><strong>L6</strong> — Observing techniques: spectroscopy</li>
    <li><strong>P2</strong> — Project 2: HERMES data analysis</li>
    <li><strong>L7</strong> — Observing techniques: interferometry (Peter Chingaipe)</li>
    <li><strong>L8</strong> — Observing techniques: adaptive optics and turbulence</li>
    <li><strong>L9</strong> — Observing techniques: detectors (Nuno Pereira)</li>
    <li><strong>Q&amp;A</strong> — Q&amp;A session</li>
</ul>
