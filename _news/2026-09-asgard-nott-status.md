Recent papers report progress on [Asgard/NOTT](https://fys.kuleuven.be/ster/research-projects/nott/nott-asgard/), the L-band nulling instrument we are building for the VLTI, and on the broader [Asgard](http://asgard-vlti.org/) suite.

On the KU Leuven four-beam test bench, T. Mattheussen and colleagues report the first spectrally dispersed ambient nulls, a revised acquisition and calibration pipeline, and a first look at the splitting ratios of the Gallium Lanthanum Sulfide photonic chip. Those measurements complement earlier broadband cryogenic chip tests. The present ambient contrast is still well short of the on-sky goal; next steps include intensity matching to the chip’s couplers before repeating the assessment with the final cryostat and camera.

Peter Chingaipe reports on the status and performance of the NOTT cold nulling camera, the HAWAII-2RG system that images the photonic chip's nulled outputs at 3.5–4.0 µm. Marc-Antoine Martinod presents the integration status and plans for the full Asgard suite at the VLTI.

Read [Asgard/NOTT: Status of laboratory nulling performance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18951) (SPIE 14148, July 2026), [Status and performance of the Asgard/NOTT cold nulling camera](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21546) (SPIE 14148, 1414827), and [Pushing high angular resolution and high contrast observations on the VLTI from Y to L band with the Asgard instrumental suite: integration status and plans](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24567) (SPIE 14148, 1414808).
